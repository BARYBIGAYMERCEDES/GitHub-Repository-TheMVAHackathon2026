# MVA-PRISM Track 2

## Biomarker-gated drug repurposing for candidate BUB1B-mediated Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy

**Participant:** BARYBIGAY  
**Institution:** Instituto ChromoMED, Santo Domingo, Dominican Republic  
**Investigator:** Bary G. Bigay Mercedes, MD, MSc, MBA, IFCAP

> **Research-use statement:** The medicines discussed here are repurposing hypotheses for experimental validation. This report is not a treatment recommendation and does not establish safety or efficacy in MVA.

## Executive summary

Track 1 prioritized a candidate biallelic BUB1B model: GRCh38 chr15:40209701 T>G (NM_001211.6:c.2210T>G; p.Leu737Ter) plus chr15:40220612 T>G (NM_001211.6:c.3006T>G; p.Asn1002Lys). Both are heterozygous PASS calls and phase remains unresolved. The first is ClinVar-pathogenic for MVA1; the second is a kinase-domain missense VUS.

The lead Track 2 proposal is **nicotinic acid (niacin)** as an approved NAD+ precursor to test whether NAD+-SIRT2 signaling can increase residual BUBR1 abundance and improve spindle-checkpoint function. A secondary branch evaluates **rapalog-class mTORC1 inhibition** only if patient-derived cells demonstrate pathway hyperactivation.

## Mechanistic chain

BUB1B -> BUBR1 deficiency/instability -> impaired spindle assembly checkpoint and kinetochore error correction -> chromosome missegregation -> mosaic aneuploidy -> cancer and multisystem phenotype.

The candidate genotype is topologically similar to a published MVA architecture combining a truncating allele near residue 753 with a kinase-domain missense near residue 1012. This is supportive analogy, not proof of functional equivalence.

## Candidate 1: nicotinic acid (niacin)

Nicotinic acid can increase cellular NAD+ through NAPRT. SIRT2 is NAD+-dependent, and prior work showed that SIRT2/NAD+ signaling can increase BUBR1 abundance; NMN increased BUBR1 abundance in mice. The hypothesis is that approved nicotinic acid could reproduce the abundance-rescue component in patient-derived cells if the p.Asn1002Lys allele produces residual unstable protein.

**Go criteria:** increased BUBR1 protein, improved spindle-checkpoint response, fewer lagging chromosomes/micronuclei, and lower aneuploidy burden without excessive cytotoxicity.

**Falsification:** no increase in BUBR1 or no improvement in chromosome-segregation fidelity.

## Candidate 2: biomarker-gated mTORC1 inhibition

Published BUBR1 truncation models have shown allele-specific mTORC1 hyperactivation and sarcopenia. Because the proband has low muscle mass/skeletal muscle atrophy, p-S6, p-p70S6K and p-4EBP1 should be measured first. Only if elevated should a rapalog be tested ex vivo. Everolimus is a practical translational candidate because of pediatric regulatory experience; sirolimus is a useful mechanistic comparator.

This branch is secondary because mTOR inhibition may suppress growth and immunity and has not been shown to correct the primary BUBR1 checkpoint defect.

## Validation pipeline

1. Confirm both variants and establish phase.
2. Establish baseline BUBR1 abundance, SAC function, chromosome missegregation and aneuploidy in patient-derived cells.
3. Test niacin for target engagement, BUBR1 abundance and functional rescue.
4. Measure mTORC1 biomarkers; test a rapalog only if the pathway is hyperactive.
5. Repeat in independent cultures/passages with blinded scoring.
6. Consider translation only after independent ethics/regulatory review and pediatric safety oversight.
